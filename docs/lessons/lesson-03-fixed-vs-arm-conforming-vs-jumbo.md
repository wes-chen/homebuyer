# Lesson 3: Fixed vs ARM, conforming vs jumbo

Every mortgage answers two questions. One: how does the rate behave — fixed
or adjustable? Two: how big is the loan — small enough for Fannie Mae and
Freddie Mac to buy (conforming), or bigger (jumbo)? The choices are
independent: a fixed-rate jumbo and an adjustable conforming loan both exist.

**Fixed-rate** loans never move. The 30-year fixed is America's default
mortgage: predictable, priced a touch higher because the lender
absorbs the rate risk. The 15-year fixed charges a lower rate but a much
larger payment.

**ARMs** (adjustable-rate mortgages) stay fixed for a while, then float. The
common Bay Area shape is the 7/6 ARM: seven years at a fixed intro rate —
usually below the 30-year fixed — then it adjusts every six months off an
index plus a margin, with caps on each jump and a lifetime ceiling. The bet:
you'll move, sell, or refinance before year seven. If you're still holding in
year eight and rates are higher, the payment can jump hard — so model the
payment at the lifetime cap, not the teaser rate. They survive in the Bay
Area because at $2M prices, a half-point cheaper intro rate is hundreds a
month in the early years — a bet on your timeline, not a free lunch.

**Conforming vs jumbo** is about loan size. For 2026 the FHFA set the
national line at $832,750 — but Santa Clara and San Mateo counties are
high-cost areas, so the local line is the high-cost ceiling: $1,249,125.
Loans at or under it can be sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac —
standardized underwriting, deep investor demand, usually the best rates.
Above it you're in **jumbo**: the lender holds the risk, so underwriting
gets stricter (higher credit bar, bigger cash reserves) and the rate often
runs a touch higher — jumbo rates have lately printed above conforming.

Same scale as lessons 1–2: $2M home, 20% down, $1.6M loan — deep into jumbo
territory. At ~7.5% (the 30-year fixed as of Oct 8, 2026 — rates move
weekly, so treat any rate number as perishable), principal and interest run
about $11,200/month; at ~7% (a typical ARM intro), about $10,600. That
~$550/month gap is the price of certainty.

The FIRE tie-in: at 7.5%, year-one interest is roughly $120,000 — more than
half a year's ~$200k savings, redirected from compounding toward $5M-by-40
straight to the lender. Every half-point on the rate moves roughly $8,000 a
year from your future to the bank. Mortgage choice is a FIRE decision
wearing a housing costume.

**Action:** get three side-by-side quotes — the 30-year fixed, a 7/6 ARM,
and the ARM's payment at its lifetime cap.
