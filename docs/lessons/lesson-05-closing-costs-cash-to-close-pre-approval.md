# Lesson 5: Closing costs, cash to close, and pre-approval

The down payment isn't the last check you write. There's a second bill — closing costs — and a piece of paper you need before you can even bid: the pre-approval.

**Closing costs** are the fees for the loan, the title transfer, and the transaction itself. Budget 2–5% of the purchase price — on the $2M example from lessons 1–4, roughly $40k–$100k, Bay Area deals typically ~2–3%. Three buckets:

- **Lender fees.** Origination (often ~1% of the loan), underwriting, appraisal, credit report. Section A of the Loan Estimate is the comparison zone — identical loans can carry very different Section A totals.
- **Title and escrow.** Lender's title insurance (required), escrow fee, recording, notary. Northern California custom splits these — escrow fees commonly 50/50, the seller usually pays the owner's title policy and the county transfer tax ($1.10 per $1,000 of price in CA, ~$2,200 on $2M), the buyer pays the lender's policy and recording. Negotiable; the purchase contract spells out the split.
- **Prepaids.** The first year's insurance and several months of property tax funded into escrow at closing (CA taxes due Dec 10 and Apr 10), plus prepaid interest for the days between closing and month-end.

**Cash to close** is the single number that matters: down payment + closing costs − the earnest money deposit already paid − any seller credits. On the $2M example with 20% down: ~$400k down + ~$50k in costs, minus a 1–3% earnest deposit. The Closing Disclosure must arrive 3 business days before signing — compare it line by line against the Loan Estimate; tolerance rules cap how much most fees can move.

**Pre-approval vs pre-qualification.** Pre-qualification is an estimate from self-reported numbers — unverified, and sellers ignore it. Pre-approval means the lender verified your credit, income, and assets; it's underwritten and usually comes with a rate-lock window. In Bay Area bidding, pre-approval is table stakes — many listing agents won't take an offer seriously without one. Get it *before* touring seriously, then freeze your finances until the loan funds: no new credit lines, no big purchases, no job changes. Any of those can kill the approval.

The FIRE tie-in: cash to close is lump money that leaves invested capital in one motion. On the $2M example, $450k+ at the table is more than two years of ~$200k/yr savings — gone from compounding the day it wires. Every $250k the house shrinks, roughly $55k–60k stays invested instead. The decision isn't just the monthly payment from lesson 4, it's the decades of compounding this cash would otherwise earn.

**Action:** when you're ready, get Loan Estimates from two lenders on the same loan — you don't need a house picked out. Comparing Section A fees is the highest-return hour in this process.
