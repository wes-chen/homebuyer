# Lesson 9: Comps: how to tell if a price is real

A list price is an asking price, not a value. In the Bay Area it's often a starting bid, so the only way to know what a house is worth is comps — comparable homes that actually sold, recently, nearby. Your agent will pull a CMA (comparative market analysis); this lesson is so you can read it critically.

**What counts as a comp.** A good comp sold — not listed, not pending — within the last 3–6 months, within roughly a mile (same neighborhood or school boundary if you can), with similar beds, baths, square footage (within about 20%), lot size, age, and condition. Three to five solid comps beat ten loose ones.

**Price per square foot is a starting point, not a verdict.** Divide each comp's sold price by its living area and you get a $/sq ft range. Santa Clara County's median is about $1,113/sq ft as of November 17, 2026 (MLSListings) — but that blends Cupertino with East San Jose, so your comp set's range is what matters. Then adjust: a remodeled kitchen sells higher than a fixer; a busy street sells lower than a cul-de-sac; a bigger lot, a view, a better school boundary all move the number. Appraisers adjust line by line, usually $10k–$50k per feature; the direction matters more than the exact size.

**Read the sale, not just the price.** Sold in 8 days with 12 offers, the market paid up; sat 60 days and sold below list, the price was aspirational. Check the sale-to-list ratio (sold price ÷ list price) on each comp: in a hot micro-market it runs above 100%; when it dips below, the list price was the ceiling, not the floor. Pending sales are the freshest signal of where the market is heading.

**The trap: cherry-picked comps.** The classic seller move is pricing off the neighborhood's best sale — the remodeled house on the best street — and ignoring the three ordinary sales next door. If your comps imply a value well below list, the list price is a wish — anchor your offer to the comps, not the ask. The reverse is also true: if every comp supports the price, paying at or above list may be fair. Comps protect you both ways. An overpay isn't abstract: every $100k above true value is $100k of equity that stops compounding.

**Action:** pick a listing you like and ask your agent (or use the sold filter on Redfin/Zillow) for its 3–5 closest sold comps. Compute the $/sq ft range, note one adjustment per comp, and write down the value the comps imply versus list. That's where your offer in lesson 10 starts.
