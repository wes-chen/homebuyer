# Lesson 4: The true monthly cost

The mortgage payment is not the monthly cost of owning a home — it's about
two-thirds of it. Lenders quote you PITI (principal, interest, taxes,
insurance), and that's what the 28/36 rule from Lesson 1 runs on. But the
taxes, the insurance, and the maintenance your house quietly demands every
year are real money too — and maintenance is the line item first-time
buyers chronically miss.

**T is property tax.** California's base is 1% of the purchase price; Prop 13
locks your assessed value to that, rising at most 2% a year. Local bonds and
parcel taxes stack on top, so in Santa Clara and San Mateo counties the
all-in effective rate usually lands around 1.1–1.2% — verify any specific
address on the county assessor's site. On the $2M example home from lessons
1–3, ~1.18% is $23,600/year, about $1,970/month, before you pay a cent of
mortgage interest.

**I is homeowners insurance.** Statewide analyses put California averages
around $130–$215/month in 2026 (dated October 2026 — rising fast as carriers
reprice California risk; hillside fire zones can land much higher, or push
you onto the FAIR Plan). For a $2M Bay Area house with real dwelling
coverage, expect roughly $250–$350/month — and this number is
property-specific, so get actual quotes once you have an address, not at
closing.

Then the invisible line item: **maintenance.** Roofs, water heaters, plumbing,
paint, the HVAC system that dies in August. The rule of thumb is ~1% of the
home's value per year — on our $2M example that's $20,000/year, about
$1,670/month. Older homes run hotter, newer cooler. Skipping it is just a
loan you take out from the house itself, at the worst possible terms.

Put it together at the lesson-3 rate (~7.5% 30-year fixed, dated Oct 8,
2026): P&I ~$11,200 + tax ~$1,970 + insurance ~$300 + maintenance ~$1,670 ≈
**~$15,100/month — about $181,000 a year.** (HOA is the silent fifth letter,
but only if you buy one: most Bay Area single-family homes have none. If a
planned community charges one, it stacks on top of everything above.)

The FIRE tie-in: ~$181k a year is roughly 90% of his ~$200k/yr savings. The
mortgage rate isn't the lever — the house is. Every $250k less house removes
about $22k a year from this number; every $250k more adds it. This is the
tradeoff he already accepted: money that becomes home equity stops
compounding, so the price tag is the FIRE-date dial.

**Action:** for any house you tour, build this five-line budget before you
fall in love with the listing price — and shop insurance the week you get
serious, not the week of closing.
