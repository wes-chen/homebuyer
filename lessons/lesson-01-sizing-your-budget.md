# Lesson 1: How to size your budget

Two numbers decide everything: what you can carry per month, and what cash
you need on day one.

The monthly side runs on the **28/36 rule**, the standard guideline lenders
use. At most 28% of your gross monthly income goes to housing (mortgage +
property tax + insurance — "PITI"), and at most 36% goes to all debts
combined, housing included. It's not a law, but it's the math behind every
pre-approval.

A Bay Area worked example, so the scale lands: a $2M home with 20% down
($400k) means a $1.6M loan. At ~6.5% on a 30-year fixed, principal and
interest run about $10,100/month. Add California property tax (~1.18%
effective, roughly $1,970/month on $2M — Prop 13 then caps its growth at
2%/year) and homeowner's insurance (~$250/month). Total PITI: roughly
**$12,300/month**. Under the 28% rule that implies ~$44k/month gross income,
~$528k/year.

The cash side: down payment plus **closing costs** — lender fees, title,
escrow, prepaid taxes — typically 1.5–2.5% of the price in California. On
that $2M home: ~$400k down + ~$30–50k closing = roughly **$440k cash to
close**. Less than 20% down is possible (lesson 2, with a monthly PMI
surcharge), but 20% is the clean baseline.

Action: pull three numbers — gross monthly income, total monthly debt
payments, liquid savings. Lesson 2 turns those into an actual price range.

_General education, not financial advice — a lender's pre-approval gives the
real number when the time comes (lesson 5)._
